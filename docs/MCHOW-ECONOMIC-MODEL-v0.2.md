## 1. Gates and conventions

- **Hard gate:** a $1,000 MCHOW trade must have ≤ 5.0% **average execution** price impact (fee excluded), in **both** directions, at launch **and in every month of the reward program** (so at stressed prices). Preferred ≤ 2.0%.
- Independent LP value share ≥ 25.0% by month 3; preferred ≥ 40.0% by month 6. These are post-launch milestones, not pre-launch facts.
- **Convention correction to v0.1.** v0.1's launch table reported how far the *spot price* moves; execution impact (how much worse the whole trade fills than spot) is about half of that. Under the execution convention a full-range launch position needs:

| Trade | ≤ 5% execution impact | ≤ 2% execution impact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $500 | $9,500 of WETH | $24,500 of WETH |
| $1,000 | $19,000 of WETH | $49,000 of WETH |

These hold only **at the launch price**. When MCHOW falls by a factor k, a full-range position's WETH side shrinks by √k, so depth in dollars falls; the program-wide gate below captures that.

- Uniswap protocol fee: Base V3 pools at 0.30% and 1% were read on-chain with `feeProtocol` = 1/6, so LPs keep 83.3% of the headline fee. The MCHOW pool's own setting must be read when it is created.
- UniswapV3Staker periods last at most 24 months, so 48-, 60-, and 72-month programs run as 2, 3, and 3 consecutive incentives.

## 3a. Can the depth gate and the independent-share gate pass together?

At the minimum WETH that passes the depth gate, the launch position is worth about 2 × that WETH. Outside LPs can hold at most their capital ceiling (LP count × average position), so the best possible independent share at launch prices is `ceiling ÷ (ceiling + launch value)`. Experiment A60 shown. In the conservative scenario the other experiments need $35,700–$39,800, and in stress $55,400–$77,600; every one of them still leaves the best possible share far below 25%.

| Scenario | Outside-LP ceiling | Min WETH (depth gate) | Best possible independent share | Gate ≥ 25% by m3 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| stress | $5,250 | $69,046 | 3.7% | **impossible** |
| conservative | $11,250 | $39,823 | 12.4% | **impossible** |
| base | $26,250 | $19,000 | 40.9% | possible |
| strong | $75,000 | $19,000 | 66.4% | possible |

In the stress and conservative scenarios no configuration tested can pass both gates: the WETH needed for depth makes the protocol position too large for the expected outside LPs ever to reach a 25% share. Passing both needs either far more outside LP capital than those scenarios assume, or depth that does not come mainly from the protocol's own position.

## 6. Findings (with the assumptions in §7; re-read the tables if those change)

1. **Depth alone is affordable; viability is not.** The hard depth gate passes from about $19,000 of WETH in the base and strong scenarios and about $36,000–$40,000 in the conservative scenario (around $55,000–$78,000 in stress). But at those budgets the conservative and stress cases still lose most of their price, outside LPs leave, and the independent-share gate cannot be met (§3a).
2. **No economically viable configuration emerges under conservative or stress assumptions.** Every experiment fails the independent-share gate there, and outside LP capital is near zero six months after rewards end in every scenario, including strong adoption: there is no evidence yet of liquidity that survives declining incentives.
3. **The binding constraint is net demand, not emissions timing.** In the sensitivity table, moving net organic flow from −2% to +2% of volume swings the end price from about −94% to −52%; volume, LP counts, sell propensity, and the hurdle barely move it.
4. **Slower emissions (A48–A72) and smaller reserves (B) mostly transfer risk.** They soften the price fall to month 24 but cut each month's reward, so fewer LPs come and fewer rewards are claimable; end prices in weak scenarios are similar. A smaller community allocation (C) helps price modestly. The declining curve (D) is weakest for LP participation.
5. **Month 24 is a real trade-off.** Retaining the launch position keeps a $1,000 trade near 2% impact but leaves community LPs only a small claimable share; removing it raises claimable rewards and outside-LP retention but breaks the depth gate (impact jumps to 11–17%).
6. **The locked position dominates fees as well as rewards.** Because the protocol position is most of the in-range liquidity, it earns most trading fees, which is a further reason outside LPs leave when rewards stop.

These results argue against setting a launch budget. They point to a design question to resolve first: where durable demand for MCHOW comes from, and whether depth can come from independent providers rather than mainly from the project's own position.

## 7. Assumptions

| Scenario | Gross volume/mo (range) | Outside LPs (range) | Net flow / volume | LP hurdle | Rewards sold | Overhang sold/mo |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| stress | $10,000 ($5,000–$15,000) | 3.5 (2–5) | -4.0% | 80.0% | 100.0% | 25.0% |
| conservative | $32,500 ($15,000–$50,000) | 7.5 (5–10) | 0.0% | 60.0% | 90.0% | 15.0% |
| base | $100,000 ($50,000–$150,000) | 17.5 (10–25) | 1.0% | 40.0% | 70.0% | 10.0% |
| strong | $325,000 ($150,000–$500,000) | 50 (25–75) | 2.0% | 30.0% | 50.0% | 6.0% |

- Volume is flat (no growth assumed). Net organic flow is a share of gross volume, so high volume can coexist with net selling. Outside-LP capital is capped at LP count × $1,500.
- Released team and community tokens join an unsold **overhang**; each month a scenario-specific share of the overhang is sold. Release is not sale. Treasury, constrained reserves, and unpaired launch tokens are never sold. Proceeds leave the pool.
- Community LPs: ±50.0% ranges (5.45× full-range efficiency), in range 75.0% of the time. Capital moves toward the most that still earns the hurdle: 40.0% of the gap per month entering, 70.0% leaving. Rewards valued at 75.0% of spot. LPs absorb half the estimated impermanent loss in capital.
- The locked launch position counts against community reward share (UniswapV3Staker uses all in-range liquidity). Dead-address totals include unclaimed rewards and, for D, the never-scheduled remainder.
- 1.00% fee pools keep 50% of the volume of a 0.30% pool (assumed). LP fee income is after the 1/6 protocol fee.
- Not modelled: finalization delay, V3 tick-by-tick depth of individual positions, arbitrage with other pools, MEV, gas, demand reacting to price, reward vesting.
