## 3. Cost of the two-hop route

A buyer coming from WETH to a MCHOW-quoted token pays the project pool's 1.0% fee plus MCHOW/WETH's 0.3% fee and that pool's price impact; a WETH-quoted launch pays only the project pool. Extra cost of the MCHOW route at the launch depth of the conservative minimum WETH:

| Trade | Extra fee | Extra price impact on MCHOW/WETH | Total extra cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| $100 | 0.3% | 0.4% | 0.7% |
| $500 | 0.3% | 2.1% | 2.4% |
| $1,000 | 0.3% | 4.1% | 4.4% |
| $5,000 | 0.3% | 17.6% | 17.9% |

Small trades pay little extra; large trades pay materially more unless the canonical pool is deep. Routing is only competitive while that extra cost stays small relative to the value of the launch.

## 6. Who benefits

- **The DEX:** the launchpad charges no platform fee at the baseline, so the platform earns nothing from launches in any scenario here. Any platform fee would be a new design decision and a new legal question.
- **Outside LPs:** the fee APY column in §2 shows their return from fees alone once rewards stop; viability requires it to meet their hurdle.
- **MCHOW holders:** the price columns. Seed buying is the only sustained source of net buying the launchpad adds; leak-back of project-pool MCHOW offsets part of it as launched tokens fade.

## 7. Findings (with the assumptions in §8; re-read the tables if those change)

1. **The launchpad target is concrete.** With organic MCHOW trading at $32,500/month, reaching $220,000/month through MCHOW/WETH needs about $375,000/month of launched-token trading if half of it routes through MCHOW ($12,500/day), $250,000 at 75%, and $750,000 at 25% (§1).
2. **A busy launchpad makes R3 viable even under stress organic assumptions; a steady one does in the base case.** The hypothetical “busy” launchpad (15 launches a month, about $3M/month of launched-token trading) passes every criterion in all three organic scenarios with about $9,500 of launch WETH. “Steady” (5 a month, about $500,000) passes in the base scenario and, in the conservative scenario, fails only the LP-retention criterion. “Slow” (2 a month) is not enough.
3. **The LP-retention criterion partly measures overshoot.** Rewards attract more outside LP capital than fees alone can support; when rewards stop, capital falls back to what fees support. In base + steady at 50–75% routed and conservative + steady at 75–100% routed, the LPs who remain earn about their hurdle from fees and $5,000–$18,000 of outside liquidity stays, so that remainder is sustainable even though the retention ratio fails. In conservative + steady at 25–50% routed, outside LPs leave almost entirely and fees pay below their hurdle. A criterion based on fee-supported outside capital after rewards may fit better than a ratio; that is a decision to make.
4. **Seed buying is what moves the price, and it dominates the price outcome.** Launch creators buying MCHOW to seed their pools is the only sustained net buying the launchpad adds; in the busy and steady cases it outweighs reward selling, team unlocks, and leak-back, so the price rises. That result is only as good as the launch-count and seed-size assumptions.
5. **Two hops cost large trades a lot at minimum depth.** At the conservative minimum WETH, routing through MCHOW adds about 0.7% to a $100 trade but 4.4% to $1,000 and 17.9% to $5,000 (§3). Large trades will avoid MCHOW-quoted tokens unless MCHOW/WETH is deeper than the launch minimum, which lowers the realistic routed share.
6. **The ¼×–4× range survives moderate stress but not heavy stress.** Extra selling of up to $2,000/month for six months keeps the price inside the range (lowest −68%); $5,000/month drives it below ¼×, where the locked position adds no depth for 51 months and a $1,000 trade costs about 44% (§4).
7. **Heavier reward selling changes little under R3** (§5): with the community allocation folded into rewards, reward selling is a smaller share of total selling.
8. **The DEX earns nothing.** With no platform fee, the launchpad can succeed for MCHOW holders and LPs while producing no revenue for the platform (§6).
